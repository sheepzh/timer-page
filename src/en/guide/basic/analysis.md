# {{$frontmatter.title}}

Tracking tells you where the time went; the reports turn that raw data into something you can act on. Beyond the basic per-domain records, the dashboard offers two analytical views: a habit report about your browsing in general, and a site report about one website you select. Both are built entirely on the time and visit counts already recorded on your device, so nothing is sent anywhere to produce them.

## Habit report

The habit report summarizes your browsing across all sites. It consists of two parts.

-   **Website usage habits.** This part shows your most-visited content ranked by total time, and breaks the distribution down as a pie chart, so you can see at a glance which few sites take the largest share of your browsing.
-   **Time period habits.** This part shows when you browse: your busiest hours as a time-of-day distribution, and your longest idle periods. Together they point at the times when your attention tends to drift, which is often more useful than the daily total alone.

You can filter these views by domain, by date or by hour of the day. The grouping respects the same rules described in [Track Website Time](/en/guide/basic/stat): merged domains are counted together, while custom sites and tags let you analyze a single subpage or a labeled group on its own.

## Site report

Select a single website to open its site report. Likewise, it consists of two parts.

-   **Summary.** Overview data for the selected site: the total browsing time and visit count for the period you are looking at.
-   **Trends.** The historical trend of viewing time and number of visits per day for that website, so you can tell whether a site is a steady habit or a one-off spike.

## Compare periods

Both reports support period comparison, so you can put this week next to last week, or any two ranges, and see whether your browsing on a site is going up or down. This is the clearest way to check whether a change you made actually had an effect.

## From reports to action

The reports are diagnostic: they point at the sites and the hours that cost you the most. When a number stands out, you can act on it directly with a [website time limit](/en/guide/basic/limit) in the same extension, and you can always [export the underlying data](/en/guide/data) as JSON or CSV to analyze it yourself.
