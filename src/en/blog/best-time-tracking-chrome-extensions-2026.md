# The Best 6 Time Tracking Chrome Extensions in 2026

Most browsing is a series of quick checks that quietly turn into long sessions. Nothing records that time by default, so the daily total usually comes as a surprise. A time tracking Chrome extension fixes that: it counts how long you spend on each website on its own, with no manual timers.

There is one catch when picking one. Most extensions cover only part of the job. Some only count time, some only show charts, some only block sites, and using three extensions for one workflow is its own kind of clutter. This list favors tools that combine tracking with analysis or site control, and marks clearly which extensions do what.

The list below covers the best time tracking Chrome extensions in 2026. All six were selected from the Chrome Web Store by comparing publicly available information: store ratings and user counts, feature lists, privacy disclosures, and the source repositories of the open-source projects. Ratings and user counts are refreshed monthly from the store listings.

Our selection criteria:

- **All-in-one capability**: can it track, analyze, and help you take action?
- **Accuracy** of time tracking across tabs and windows
- **Privacy**: does it keep data local, or ship your browsing history to remote servers?
- **Analytical depth**: reports that go beyond raw per-site totals
- **Ease of use**: install and it works, no manual configuration required
- **Pricing transparency**: no surprise paywalls, no ads, no hidden costs

## 1. Time Tracker - Web Habit Builder

**The all-in-one option: tracking, reports and site limits in one extension.**

<a href="https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/time-tracker-web-habit-bu/dkdhhcbjijekmneelocdllcldcpmekmm" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Time Tracker</a> is an open-source extension (MIT licensed) that combines time tracking, habit analysis and website limiting. On the Chrome Web Store it holds a 4.9-star rating from 134 reviews with over 10,000 users; on Firefox Add-ons it is listed as a Top Rated extension.

Most trackers on this list cover one part of the workflow: counting, charting or blocking. Time Tracker puts all three in a single interface, which is the main reason it ranks first here.

### Core Advantages

- **Track, analyze, and limit in one place.** Time is recorded automatically per site. Habit reports summarize your most-visited content, busiest periods and longest idle times. On top of the reports you can set rules: daily and weekly limits, per-visit limits, forbidden time periods and focus sessions for any URL pattern, so you can limit social media time on Chrome without extra tools.
- **Automatic tracking with no setup.** After installation the extension records time per website on its own, and the toolbar icon shows the time for the current site. That makes it an automatic screen time tracker for Chrome: no timers to start or stop.
- **Rules with real enforcement.** When a limit is reached the page is covered by a lock screen, and you can require a question, a password or two-factor verification to unlock the rule. This makes the limits harder to skip in a weak moment.
- **Local storage only.** Data stays in `chrome.storage.local`; nothing is sent to the developer's servers. Export is available as JSON or CSV, and data can be imported from Webtime Tracker, Web Activity Time Tracker and History Trends Unlimited. It works as a time tracker Chrome extension with no account required.

### Additional Features

- Smart domain merging rules to combine related sites (e.g., all Google subdomains) into unified statistics
- Data sync via your own GitHub Gist, WebDAV server or Obsidian for cross-device usage
- Available in 14 languages including English, Spanish, French, German, Japanese, Chinese, and more
- Lightweight at about 1 MB

### Limitations

- Tracking covers browser activity only; time spent in desktop applications is out of scope
- No built-in cloud service: cross-device sync requires your own Gist, WebDAV server or Obsidian, configured by you
- Reports are based on visit data; there is no productivity scoring or automatic site categorization like RescueTime offers
- On Android it runs only in Firefox, and site blocking on mobile arrived in v4.1.0

### Best For

Anyone who wants tracking, reports and site limits in one free tool, and prefers to keep browsing data on the device.

### Pricing

**Free and open source (MIT).** No ads, no account, no premium tier.

## 2. Web Activity Time Tracker

**An open-source tracker with basic limiting capabilities.**

<a href="https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/web-activity-time-tracker/hhfnghjdeddcfegfekjeihfmbjenlomm" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Web Activity Time Tracker</a> is an open-source Chrome extension that provides straightforward time tracking with a clean interface. It reports over 30,000 users and a 4.83-star rating on the store.

### Core Advantages

- **Open-source codebase** hosted on GitHub, so the privacy claims can be verified in the code
- **Daily visit limits** that block access to sites once your self-imposed threshold is reached
- **Clean dashboard** displaying time spent per domain with simple bar charts
- **Lightweight** with minimal impact on browser performance

### Limitations

- Analysis is limited to basic time-per-domain views without habit reports
- No per-visit time limits or scheduled blocking periods
- Limited data export options
- No cross-device sync

### Best For

Users who prioritize open-source transparency and need basic time awareness without advanced analytics.

### Pricing

**Free and open source.**

## 3. Toggl Track

**The team-oriented option with 100+ app integrations.**

<a href="https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/toggl-track/oejgccbfbmkkhai8nfoo9lipp0hclgo" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Toggl Track</a> is one of the most widely known time tracking services, designed for freelancers and teams who log billable hours. Its Chrome extension adds one-click timers to over 100 popular web apps.

### Core Advantages

- **Large integration library**: track time directly within Asana, Jira, Notion, Slack, Gmail, and 100+ other tools without switching tabs
- **Pomodoro timer built in** for structured focus sessions with break reminders
- **Idle detection** that alerts you when you leave a timer running
- **Detailed reporting** for billing clients and analyzing project time allocation

### Limitations

- Designed for manual time tracking (start/stop timers), not passive browsing analysis
- Free plan limited to 5 users with 6 months of data retention
- No website blocking or access control features
- Requires an account and stores data on Toggl's servers

### Best For

Freelancers and agencies who need to track billable hours across project management tools and generate client-facing reports.

### Pricing

Free for up to 5 users (limited features). Paid plans start at **$10/user/month**.

## 4. RescueTime

**Automatic categorization with productivity scoring.**

<a href="https://www.rescuetime.com/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">RescueTime</a> takes a different approach: it automatically categorizes every website and application you use into productivity buckets, from "Very Productive" to "Very Distracting," and generates a daily productivity score.

### Core Advantages

- **Automatic productivity classification**, no manual tagging; RescueTime knows Google Docs is productive and Reddit usually is not
- **Daily productivity score** as a single metric to track over time
- **Focus sessions** that block distracting sites for a set duration
- **Works beyond the browser**: the desktop app tracks all applications, not just web activity

### Limitations

- The Chrome extension alone is limited; full functionality requires the desktop application
- The free plan only provides basic daily summaries with 3 months of history
- Data is stored on RescueTime's servers
- Categorization can be inaccurate for niche or personal sites

### Best For

Knowledge workers who want automatic categorization and a daily score without manual effort, and are comfortable with a desktop app and cloud storage.

### Pricing

Limited free plan available. Premium costs **$12/month** (or $78/year).

## 5. Webtime Tracker

**Minimalist tracking for users who just want the basics.**

<a href="https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/webtime-tracker/ppaojnbmmaigjmlpjaldnkgnklhicppk" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Webtime Tracker</a> is the most straightforward option on this list. It tracks time per website and presents it in a pie chart.

### Core Advantages

- **Simple interface**: a single pie chart view shows your time distribution at a glance
- **Zero configuration**: install and immediately see results
- **Very lightweight** with negligible performance impact
- **Local data storage** without an account

### Limitations

- No habit reports or trend analysis
- No website blocking or limiting features
- Limited export options (CSV/JSON)
- No cross-device sync
- Development appears less active than the other projects here

### Best For

Users who want the simplest possible time awareness tool with no extra features.

### Pricing

**Free.**

## 6. Clockify

**Team time tracking with a generous free tier.**

<a href="https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/clockify-time-tracker/pmjeegjhjdlccodhacdgbgfagbpmccpe" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Clockify</a> is a team time tracking platform whose Chrome extension allows quick timer starts from the toolbar. It targets teams and freelancers who need timesheets, project tracking and team management.

### Core Advantages

- **Unlimited free tracking** for unlimited users, one of the most generous free plans in this category
- **Project and task organization** with tags for time categorization
- **Timesheets and reports** suitable for invoicing and team management
- **Tracking reminders** so you don't forget to log hours

### Limitations

- Manual timer operation only; no passive browsing tracking
- No browsing habit analysis
- No website blocking or limiting
- Requires an account with data stored on Clockify's servers

### Best For

Teams and freelancers who need a free timesheet solution for billable work hours across projects.

### Pricing

Free for unlimited users (core features). Paid plans start at **$3.99/user/month** for advanced features.
